### Red Wizard

- **Why first:** he has the best course form in the field (5th of 8, 4.0L, chasing the winner; verified), the faster of the two C&D times by about 1.8s at the same weight (≈1m27.47s v ≈1m29.30s; verified from the result pages, own times derived), and the best-franked form. The 2nd and 4th from his race have both run 2nd since and are rated 85 and 86 (verified), which puts him around 76–78 on collateral (asserted). He raced prominently last time (horses prominent last time win about 21% of these at Kempton, against 10% for those held up; verified in the 16:10 count). He went from 9th of 15 to 5th of 8 on his second run, and the stable's regular rider switches onto him (asserted as significant).
- **Strongest case against:** he has never finished closer than 4 lengths and was "outpaced 2f out" at this course and distance. His collateral rating leans on two horses whose marks came from later runs, so a weak C&D race could have been flattered. Two runs is too few to judge consistency. Twelve Bars, Fenaroli and Byzantine went past or held him, so he is not obviously a winner in waiting. A good debutant (Idealise) could simply be better.

### Stytch

- **Why second:** his best collateral, about 2L behind OR79 Gold Army at Salisbury (≈75; asserted), is close to Red Wizard's. His Racing Post figures (RPR 71, TS 42) are level with Red Wizard's (70, 44). He improved from a 12L C&D debut to 4.25L, and the first-time hood is a logical fix for the keenness.
- **Strongest case against:** he was held up in both runs and keen at Salisbury, a **recurring pattern**. Horses held up last time win about 10% of these at Kempton, half the rate of those that raced prominently. His C&D run was about 1.8s slower than Red Wizard's. His best figure came on soft turf, not Polytrack. A hood can backfire. Under Step 13 this doubt stops him being top pick.

### Idealise

- **Why third:** a Balding/Oisin Murphy newcomer with the best pedigree in the field (Frankel; dam a half-sister to Chaldean; asserted from the card). The raced form he meets is modest (best ≈75–78 collateral). In 2yo novices, the playbook rates a well-bred newcomer from a top yard highly when the exposed form is weak.
- **Strongest case against:** the exposed form is not weak at the top: Red Wizard's is franked. Newcomers win only 6.3% of Kempton 7f 2yo novices and maidens, horses with one previous run 16.2%. Balding's Kempton newcomers are 3 from 35 (9%). Pedigree and the jockey booking are not evidence of ability, which is why he is not ranked above the raced pair (the same principle the 16:10 analysis applied). Even so, he gets a well above-base 16%.

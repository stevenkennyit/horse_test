# AGENTS.md: Race Analysis (v2)

Instructions for analysing a UK/Irish flat race. Work through every step in order. Build an independent view from the data first; tipster opinion is the last input, not the first.

v2 adds lessons from reviewed race nights: what the data-gathering got wrong, and what the results showed about the reasoning. See "Post-race review" for the method.

---

## Operating directives

Read these before every analysis and hold to them throughout.

- **Max effort or fail.** Analyse the race as if a careless answer is a failed answer. Read every runner's data in full before ranking. Do not shortlist from the card, the market, or tipster consensus. If you find yourself reaching for a quick conclusion, that is the signal to slow down and go back to the data. "It probably wins" is not the standard; "every claim is true and checked" is.

- **Read everything, entirely.** Open and read the actual run history, result pages and profiles for each runner, not summaries of them. A figure, comment or form string you have not read at source is not evidence yet. A search snippet is not a source.

- **Critically review and challenge your own claims.** After producing an analysis, actively argue against it. For each selection, write the strongest case that it is wrong. Treat your own confident phrasing as the thing most likely to be hiding an error.

- **Critically review the data and the selections, separately.** Audit the *data* (is each figure what the source actually says?) and the *selections* (does the ranking follow from the data, or from a conclusion you reached early?) as two distinct passes. A sound-looking pick built on a misread figure fails both.

- **A win does not validate the process.** A correct selection resting on an unverified or false assumption is a process failure, not a success. In low-grade, open racing almost anything can win; being right by luck is not being right. Judge the work by whether the reasoning was true and checked, independent of the result.

---

## Core factors (weight these most heavily)

Three factors carry the most weight in every analysis:

1. **Weight change**: what the horse carried in its key past runs compared with what it carries today.
2. **Finishing times**: the horse's actual times in similar races, adjusted for weight.
3. **Consistency**: how reliably the horse runs to its level across recent runs.

Ratings (RPR, Topspeed), draw and connections are secondary. **Prices and market moves are not inputs** — the agent ranks on the race data alone and does not fetch or quote odds. Use them to support or challenge the core picture, not to override it.

**Head-to-heads are the strongest single evidence when they repeat.** When the same horses have met in several races and the weight-adjusted results agree, trust that over any single time figure or rating. Repeated head-to-heads have held in results where a top pick chosen on one fast time from another course has not.

## Every runner, in full

Analyse **every runner in the race** through every step, not just the favourites or the horses that look likely on the card. Do not shortlist before the data is in.
- Big-priced horses, handicap debutants and horses with poor-looking form figures get the same full treatment.
- Form figures, prices and tipster consensus hide things: a well-handicapped horse on its best weight, a fast time buried under a bad finishing position, or a favourite whose consistency is weaker than it looks.
- Only rank after every runner's run history, weight change, times and consistency have been recorded.
- If time runs short, say which runners were not fully checked rather than quietly skipping them.

## Claims discipline

Every statement in a summary, grid or rationale is a factual claim. Most analytical errors happen here, not in gathering — the research is done well and then degraded when written up. Hold the write-up to the same rigour as the research.

- **Write rationale from the data, never from your conclusion.** Before stating why a horse is ranked where it is, re-read that runner's recorded data. The rationale must be derived from the table in front of you, not reconstructed from memory of the ranking you already chose. If the justification came to mind before you re-checked the data, check it before writing it.

- **Superlatives require a full-field scan.** "Only," "best," "fastest," "most consistent," "highest figure," "the sole X" — each is falsified by a single counter-example. Never write one without scanning every runner's data to confirm it. If you have not checked all of them, do not use the word; say "one of the …" or name the specific comparison instead. A "best time" can turn out to be joint-best when a second runner's figure is within a few hundredths.

- **State figures at the precision you actually hold.** If a rating shows as 73 on one source and 75 on another, write "73–75," not "75." Do not round up confidence. Where today's weight or mark depends on the exact figure, the imprecision matters — flag it rather than paper over it.

- **Count, don't estimate.** Losing runs, runs since a win, places in the last six: count them from the run list. An estimated "12 runs without a win" has turned out to be 9 when counted.

- **Grade every claim: verified / asserted / not found.**
  - *Verified* — confirmed at source this session.
  - *Asserted* — stated from memory or inference; go back and verify before it appears in a selection's rationale.
  - *Not found* — searched and absent. Say "no faster time found," not "slowest." Absence of evidence is weaker than evidence of absence, and the wording must show it.

- **When sources conflict, report both and say which you rely on and why.** Two independent sources agreeing beats one. Racing Post result pages beat profile pages, cards and snippets.

## Step 1: Pull the racecard

For every runner, record:
- Draw, today's weight (and whether any apprentice claim is already deducted), official rating (OR), RPR, Topspeed
- Form figures (read right to left; the most recent run is on the right)
- Days since last run
- Headgear, especially first-time (p1, b1, t1, v1, h1), and other first-time markers (w1 = first run after a breathing operation, g1 = first run since being gelded)
- Handicap debutants (Hc1)
- Rating changes already announced (future OR)
- Jockey and trainer, and any stable switch (first run for a new trainer)

Pull the card from at least two sources (Racing Post and Sporting Life). Cards disagree on jockeys, headgear and markers; record disagreements rather than picking one.

## Step 2: Pull every runner's run history

For every runner's last 6 runs (fewer if lightly raced), record in a table:

| Date | Course | Distance | Going | Class | Pos / Ran | Beaten (lengths) | Weight carried | OR | Winning time | Own time | SP |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Source: Sky Sports or Sporting Life horse profile for the run list, then the Racing Post result (or Sporting Life / At The Races as backup) for each relevant race for the winning time and time against standard.

Read the form literally. Do not accept a tipster's summary.

**Read every finishing position in context.** A position is only meaningful alongside the class, field size and strength of that race. A 4th or 6th in a stronger race can be better form than a 2nd in a weak one. Never judge a horse on its form figures (e.g. 040636) alone.

**Course and distance check.** For every runner, list every previous run:
- at today's course
- at today's distance (within about half a furlong)
- at both (course and distance)

Record position, field size and beaten distance for each. A placing at today's track is a positive signal that form figures can hide. If a card shows a C/D/CD marker you cannot match to a run, keep looking (another site, an older run) before writing "not found" — a "CD" marker that looks unsupported can be a nursery win a year or more earlier.

**"At the trip" means within about half a furlong.** A win at an adjacent distance is "at a similar trip" — state the actual distance run, do not blur it to match today's. (A 1m3f win is not "at the trip" for a 1m4f race.)

Flag:
- Layoffs over 30 days
- Beaten favourites — and how often. A horse beaten as favourite repeatedly is often a placer rather than a winner (it can still win)
- Big-priced horses beaten a long way
- Declining form trends
- **Trip changes.** A horse whose placed form is all at a different trip from today's carries a real doubt. A horse whose placed runs were all over 7f, dropped to 6f, can only run on late when it is too late.

## Step 3: Weight change (core factor)

Compare what each horse carried in its key past runs with what it carries today.

1. **Weight change since the key run.** Today's weight minus the weight carried in its best recent run and its last run. Write it as +lb or −lb. **Take any jockey's claim off before comparing** — Sporting Life result pages list weights before the claim.
2. **Convert beaten distances to pounds.** Approximate lb per length:
   - 5f: 3lb
   - 6f: 2.5lb
   - 7f: 2lb
   - 1m: 1.75lb
   - 1m2f+: 1.5lb or less
3. **Re-run past meetings at today's weights.** When two runners have met before, adjust the beaten distance for the change in weight between them.
   - Example: A beat B by 1 length over 6f (2.5lb). A now carries 3lb more relative to B than last time. On the weights, B should now finish about level with A or slightly ahead.
   - Check the claim basis is the same on both sides of the comparison (same source, both before or both after claims).
4. **Same logic against the winner of the key run.** A horse beaten 1¾ lengths over 6f (about 4lb) that is now 2lb higher is still about 6lb behind that winner on that day's figures, unless it has improved.
5. **Collateral form.** Where two runners have not met but have both met a third horse, chain the comparison through it. Mark collateral comparisons as asserted, and weaker than direct meetings.
6. **A mark below recent winning marks is a signal, not a selection.** It only helps if the horse is running to form now. A horse several pounds below old winning marks but without a win or place in its runs since is not well handicapped in practice.
7. **Flag:**
   - Horses well in at today's weights: carrying less than in a run where they ran well
   - Horses carrying significantly more than in their best run (5lb+)
   - Big rating rises after a single win (or several pounds over a winning sequence)

## Step 4: Finishing times (core factor)

Finishing times matter more than Topspeed, which is unreliable.

1. **Get each horse's own time.** Winning time plus beaten distance. At sprint distances, 1 length is about 0.2 seconds (5 lengths per second); over 1m+ about 0.17–0.2 seconds.
2. **Compare like with like.** Raw times are only directly comparable at the same course and distance, on the same surface and similar going.
3. **Across courses, compare against standard.** Express each run as seconds faster or slower than the standard (or median) time for that course and distance. Derive the standard from Racing Post results (winning time minus "slow by") and confirm it from at least two results before relying on it.
4. **Adjust for weight.** Add or subtract roughly 0.02–0.03 seconds per lb over 6f for the difference between the weight carried in that run and today's weight. A horse that ran a fast time carrying 9-9 and now carries 9-2 should run faster today, all else equal.
5. **Check the context of each time:**
   - Going on the day
   - Pace of the race (a slowly run race understates ability; a fast-run race flatters)
   - Class
   - Race type: novice and maiden races are often slowly run, so their times understate ability
6. **Read the gap.** A horse consistently faster than its rivals in similar races is a strong signal. At Class 5–6 level, 0.5–1 second (2½–5 lengths) is meaningful.
7. **One figure is not a pattern.** A single fast time — especially from a different course — is noisy in both directions. A lone best-in-field figure from another course has led both to under-ranking (a 7/1 shot that ran 2nd) and to over-ranking (a top pick that ran 5th). Rank on repeated figures and head-to-heads; use a lone figure to move a horse up or down a place, not to make it top pick.

**Note on low-grade staying races.** Class 5–6 races at 1m4f+ are routinely run many seconds slow of standard, so raw and standard-adjusted times separate runners poorly. At this grade, weight the beaten distances, how the horse was beaten, and consistency at the trip over the clock — and say so rather than pretending the time is informative.

## Step 5: Consistency (core factor)

Measure how reliably each horse runs to its level over its last 6 runs:

| Measure | How |
|---|---|
| Place rate | Top-3 finishes ÷ runs |
| Close-up rate | Runs beaten 2 lengths or less (or won) ÷ runs |
| Average beaten distance | Mean lengths behind the winner (0 for a win) |
| Spread | Best vs worst run (in lengths or weight-adjusted time) |
| Trend | Are the last 3 runs better or worse than the 3 before? |

Then judge consistency **in similar conditions** (same surface, similar distance and class). Weight these runs more than runs in different conditions.

How to use it:
- **Consistent and fast on time:** strong win candidate
- **Consistent but modest times:** place candidate. Do not promote a "consistent but modest" placer to top win pick on consistency alone — that profile places more than it wins. **Do not rank such a horse above a rival with clearly better times and a falling mark** (a consistent horse running 4s slow has been beaten out of the places by a rival with better times and a falling mark).
- **Inconsistent with one big figure:** treat the big figure with caution; it may not be repeated
- **Lightly raced (3 runs or fewer):** consistency can't be judged; mark as uncertain

## Step 6: Check ratings against results

Compare each horse's best RPR and Topspeed with its actual results, times and consistency. A high figure from a horse that keeps finishing 6–8 lengths behind at 66/1 is a false signal. Trust results and times over ratings when they disagree.

**A rating is reliable only in proportion to how it was earned.** A figure earned **in contention** — won, or beaten narrowly in a competitive field — is trustworthy. The same figure earned **finishing well beaten** is a false signal. Apply this test consistently: do not trust a high figure from one horse while dismissing it from another unless the *manner* of the run justifies the difference, and state the test when you use it.

**But "beaten narrowly" only counts if the race was strong.** In lightly raced 2yo form, an OR/RPR from a narrow defeat by an ordinary horse can rate below a heavier defeat by a high-class one. Two 2yos rated OR 81–82 from narrow defeats have been beaten by a horse rated 65 whose 6-length debut defeat came against an unbeaten horse that went on to win again.

## Step 7: Today's weight against rivals

For ratings and times, add 1 point (or about 0.025 seconds over 6f) per lb each horse receives from today's top weight. Use this to compare runners carrying different weights today. Check whether claims are already deducted from the listed weight. Apply weight-for-age where it bears on the result (e.g. a 3yo receiving from older horses), rather than reading the raw weights as level.

## Step 8: Draw and pace

Use course- and distance-specific data only. Bias at one track says nothing about another.
- Map every runner's draw against the data for this course and distance.
- Map likely running styles: who leads, who races prominently, who is held up. Check each runner's in-running comments, not just the card's label — a horse can change style (a horse held up in recent runs can make all from the widest draw).
- **Report a mixed or weak bias as neutral.** When course data conflicts or says the effect is negligible, say so. Do not upgrade a non-negative draw into a positive. Where sources disagree, note the disagreement rather than quoting the one that suits the selection.
- Examples:
  - Kempton 7f, 8+ runner handicaps: low draws best; stall 9 or higher makes winning clearly harder, and the bias strengthens in 12+ runner fields. A low draw with early pace is the ideal profile. (Geegeez, 2016–2021 data.)
  - Wolverhampton 6f, 8+ runner handicaps: stalls 1–4 win 10.7% against 5.1% for stalls 10–13, though neither group is profitable to back blind; front-runners and prominent racers clearly outperform hold-up horses. (Geegeez, 2017–Aug 2022 data.)
  - York 1m, 8+ runner handicaps: stalls 1–6 win 11.7% against 3.6% for stalls 7+. (Previously mislabelled here as Kempton 7f.)

Treat a weak bias as a tiebreaker, not a deciding factor. Note the date range of any bias figure: draw biases can weaken or reverse over time.

## Step 9: Connections

- Why is this jockey on this horse? A top jockey on a big-priced horse means more than an apprentice.
- Use course-specific trainer and jockey strike rates rather than national averages. The hot-form superscript on a racecard is a short-run meeting figure, not a course strike rate — do not let it carry more weight than it deserves. The unlabelled percentage beside a trainer on Racing Post cards is not a course strike rate either.
- Judge first-time headgear by who trains the horse: a methodical trainer (e.g. Prescott) means more than a random one.
- For 2yo maidens and novices specifically: the race pattern favours well-bred newcomers from powerful yards over exposed horses already beaten in weak races — but only when the exposed form is weak. When an exposed runner has strong form, proven ability beats unraced potential. Note which yards' debutants typically need the run (they improve next time) versus those ready first time out.

## Step 10: Non-runners and going

Close to the off:

- Re-check non-runners and re-rank if one changes the pace or the weights picture.
- Re-check the going or surface report; note any change from the card and which runners it helps or hurts.
- Record any change as a dated `## Update HH:MM` section (Step 14).

## Step 11: Summary grid

Before ranking, fill in one row for every runner in the race:

| Horse | Weight change vs key run | Weight-adjusted time vs rivals | Consistency (place rate / close-up rate / trend) | Course / distance form | Draw / pace | Connections | Key doubt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Rank mainly on the first three columns. Use the rest to separate close calls.

## Step 12: Stress-test every pick

For each selection, actively look for what's wrong with it:
- What is the strongest argument against it?
- Is the case resting on an unverified claim or a single big figure?
- Layoff, step up in class, higher weight than in its best runs, declining form?
- Any "false narrative" (e.g. "two wins in a row" when the last run was actually a 5th)?
- **Is the main doubt a specific, recurring pattern in the form?** A trip change, fading late, missing the start, keenness — if the in-running comments show it more than once, it is likely to recur. Do not make that horse top pick unless the case for it clearly outweighs the doubt. Specific doubts recorded before the off (a trip change, fading late, slow starts) have tended to recur in the race.

List everything you couldn't verify.

## Step 13: Issue the selections — Win, Each-Way, Danger

The agent's job ends at the selections. It does not recommend bets, stakes or bet types, and does not check prices; the user decides how to bet. Issue up to **three named selections, each a different horse**:

- **WIN (required):** the single most-likely winner.
- **EACH-WAY (optional):** a different horse to run into a place — the best each-way proposition *on the agent's own numbers*, normally at a longer fair price than the Win. Choose it to maximise each-way expectation (a genuine place chance at a price), **not** as "second-most-likely winner." Omit it when no runner is a worthwhile each-way proposition — a short win-only field (under 5 runners), or nothing with a place chance at a price.
- **DANGER (optional):** the chief threat to the two above — the one most likely to beat them if they don't deliver. Not a staked selection; it measures whether the shortlist caught the result. Omit it if nothing stands out.

For every selection give:
- **Win probability** (0–1) and the fair price it implies (fair decimal odds = 1 ÷ win probability; e.g. 40% → 6/4).
- **Place probability** (0–1): the chance it finishes within today's each-way places (2 for 5–7 runners, 3 for 8+, 4 for handicaps of 16+; win-only under 5).
- **Confidence** (low / medium / high), the one-line reason, and the one-line case against (for the Win and Each-Way).

The win probabilities across the field must be realistic: the three selections' win chances plus the rest of the field add up to about 100%. The **Win must hold the highest win probability**; the Each-Way usually has a lower win chance but a place chance strong enough to make it the better each-way call. **Two horses clear of the rest only on ratings is not enough** — the evidence (head-to-heads) must separate them.

- **Prices and the market are still not inputs.** "Each-way value" here means the agent's own place expectation at a longer fair price, **not** a comparison with bookmakers' odds (never seen before the off). The after-the-race SP reveals whether the Each-Way was genuinely underrated.

### How the selections are scored

After the race the Win and Each-Way earn points from the starting price (SP); the agent never sees the SP before the off. The Danger is not staked — it is scored only as coverage (was the winner one of the three?). **Stakes differ: a Win is a €1 win bet (€1 at risk); an Each-Way is a €1 each-way bet, which is €2 at risk — €1 to win and €1 to place.**

| Selection | Scored at SP |
|---|---|
| **Win** | a €1 win bet (€1 staked): wins → +win odds (10/11 → +0.9; 5/1 → +5; 16/1 → +16); anything else → −1. A Win that only places still loses. |
| **Each-Way** | a €1 each-way bet = **€2 staked** (€1 win + €1 place): **wins** → +win odds **plus** +(odds × place fraction); **placed, not won** → +(odds × place fraction) **− 1** (the lost win stake); **unplaced** → **−2**. Place fraction: a quarter of the odds in 5–7 runner races and handicaps of 12+, otherwise a fifth; win-only under 5 runners → a €1 win bet (€1 staked), scored like a Win. |
| **Danger** | not staked; counts only toward the "winner in our three" coverage rate. |

So a short-priced Win that obliges earns little, and a correctly-chosen big-priced Each-Way placer earns a lot — the **win skill is measured on the Win, the place/value skill on the Each-Way**.

**Two scoring intricacies to keep in mind.** (1) *An each-way bet costs twice a win bet.* Because €1 of it rides on the win, a **short-priced Each-Way that only places can still lose money** — the place return need not cover the lost win stake — another reason the Each-Way must be a genuinely longer-priced value pick, not a second favourite. (2) Separately, the dashboard tracks a **Selection value** diagnostic (not part of the staked score): every named pick, the Danger included, scored by the SP return of a single 1pt bet — full win odds if it won, the place fraction of the odds if it placed, −1 otherwise — to credit finding winners and big-priced placers whatever slot they were given. The footer of `dashboard.html` holds the authoritative, always-current definitions; keep this section in step with it.

- **Outsider check (required).** Before choosing, write one line for every runner outside the provisional Win/Each-Way/Danger with the best evidence-based case for it: a form line hidden by a bad run, a weight or mark drop, a change of trip, ground, course or headgear, a pace setup that suits, a stable pattern. Promote any horse whose case beats a selection. This is the main engine for finding the Each-Way; record the check in the write-up.
- **Don't choose on the obvious signals alone.** Top official rating, best recent form figures and the biggest stable or jockey are what the market prices hardest. A selection resting only on them can win and still score little; each reason must name evidence beyond them.
- **Calibrate the Win's win chance to field size and race type, not to class.** How concentrated the field's win probabilities can realistically be depends on race structure, not on how good the horses are:
  - *Small fields and non-handicaps concentrate it.* In 4–6 runner races, and in maidens, conditions and pattern races, a clear standout can fairly hold 40%+ of the win chance.
  - *Big, competitive handicaps dilute it.* Weight is set to bunch the field, so in 12+ runner handicaps (and especially 16+) no runner — however well it rates — should carry a very high win chance; a realistic Win ceiling is about 25–30%, with more probability spread across the field. This is the structural reason the outsider check — and the Each-Way — matter most in these races.
  - *Class is only a weak, confounded proxy.* Higher-class Flat favourites win marginally more often than low-grade ones (roughly 36% vs 31% in post-2020 samples), but largely because top races are more often small-field non-handicaps. Do not downgrade the Win merely because the race is low grade — read the field size and whether it is a handicap, which do the real work. *(Added 26 Oct 2026 from aggregated favourite-strike-rate studies — field size swings favourite win rates by ~15 points, handicaps run well below non-handicaps; figures are secondary, not BHA primary.)*

## Step 14: Record the analysis

Every analysed race is saved **before the off**, so the result can be judged later against what was actually said and the performance dashboard stays complete. All paths are relative to the project root `C:\Users\steven\Desktop\horse_agent`.

**1. The write-up** — one Markdown file per race:
`flat/analyses/<YYYY-MM-DD>_<course>/<HHMM>_<race-slug>.md`
(e.g. `flat/analyses/2026-11-02_kempton/1930_7f-class-5-handicap.md`). Create the meeting folder if it doesn't exist.

Required contents, in this order:
- **Header:** date, course, off time, race name, race type (handicap / novice / maiden / conditions, class), distance, going or surface, field size, draw, non-runners.
- **Analysis source:** `full` (the agent's own analysis) or `verified` (user-supplied picks checked by the agent).
- **Summary grid** (Step 11) for every runner.
- **Outsider check** (Step 13): one line for every runner outside the provisional selections.
- **Selections:** the Win, the Each-Way (if any) and the Danger (if any), each a different horse, with win probability, place probability, fair price and confidence (low / medium / high).
- **Rationale and the strongest case against** the Win and Each-Way, with key claims tagged verified / asserted / not found.
- **Unverified items** — everything that could not be checked.
- **Sources:** the URLs relied on.

If the analysis changes before the off (non-runners, going), **append** a dated `## Update HH:MM` section rather than overwriting. The record must show what was said before the race.

**2. The results data** — add the race to `flat/data/results.js` (same schema as jumps; the field notes are in the header comment of `jumps/data/results.js`). Use the existing meeting object for the date and course, or add one.

| Level | Required fields before the off |
|---|---|
| Meeting | `date`, `course`, `country`, `code: "flat"`, `playbook: "flat/AGENTS-flat-v2.md"`, `going`, `dataErrors: []` |
| Race | `time`, `name`, `type`, `distance`, `going`, `runners`, `source`, `status: "pending"`, `grade: "pending"`, `errors: []`, `notes`, `winner: null`, `writeup` (path to the write-up, e.g. `"flat/analyses/2026-10-26_kempton/1610_7f-class-4-novice-stakes.md"`), `updated` (when the picks were last checked, e.g. `"17:50 Sun 25 Oct"`) |
| Each selection | `role` (`"win"` / `"ew"` / `"danger"`), `horse`, `prob` (win probability 0–1), `placeProb` (0–1), `finish: "-"`, `confidence` (`"low"` / `"medium"` / `"high"`), `reason` (one line), `against` (one line; Win and Each-Way only). List the Win first, then the Each-Way, then the Danger. *(Legacy entries use `rank` 1/2/3 + `danger: true` and no `placeProb`; the dashboard still reads those — map rank 1 → Win, rank 2 → Each-Way, danger → Danger.)* |

`upcoming.html` shows every pending race from these fields, so keep `reason`, `against` and `updated` current with each `## Update HH:MM`.

The dashboard scores the Win and the Each-Way separately (see Step 13's scoring table), so set `role` on each selection; list the Win first.

**Do not edit picks or probabilities after the off.** Only the result fields change after the race (see Post-race review, step 6).

**Units:** probabilities 0–1; starting prices (added at review) as fractional strings exactly as returned, e.g. `"5/2"`, `"Evens"`.

## Pre-send checklist

Before any final selection, grid or rationale goes out, run these against the data you hold:

1. Is every claim in the rationale re-read from the recorded data, not written from the ranking I chose?
2. Does every superlative ("only," "best," "fastest," "most") survive a scan of all runners?
3. Is every figure stated at the precision I actually have (ranges where sources differ)?
4. Is each key claim tagged verified / asserted / not found — and are the asserted ones now verified?
5. Have I re-opened source data for anything produced after the research phase?
6. For each pick: have I written the strongest case it is wrong, and does it still stand?
7. Is any pick ranked on a single time figure from another course? Is its main doubt a recurring pattern?
8. Have I counted (not estimated) every run count I quote?
9. Have I written the outsider check, and does each pick's reason rest on more than rating, form figures and stable?
10. Is the write-up saved under `flat/analyses/…` and the race added to `flat/data/results.js` with `status: "pending"` (Step 14)?

## Post-race review

Once results are published, judge the reasoning, not the result.

1. **Pull the result from source** (Sporting Life result page usually appears within minutes; Racing Post result pages can 404 for longer).
2. **Table every ranked horse**: my rank, finishing position, beaten distance, SP, and a one-line verdict on whether the reasoning held.
3. **Separate the two kinds of error:**
   - *Data errors* — a figure, margin, headgear code or count was wrong at source or misread.
   - *Judgement errors* — the data was right but weighted wrongly (e.g. consistency over times, a single figure over repeated head-to-heads).
4. **Name what held and what failed in each race**, including correct calls that lost (a sound pick beaten narrowly or badly drawn) and wrong calls that won (one that won despite a real doubt, or for a reason the analysis didn't give).
5. **Turn each judgement error into a rule** and add it to the relevant step above. Then **log the change in `data/changelog.js`** (date, scope, title, detail) so the dashboard's "Playbook changes" can track its effect on performance.
6. **Update the record.** In `flat/data/results.js`, for each race set: `finish` and `sp` (starting price) for every pick (finish = the official position, or `UR` / `PU` / `RR` (refused to race) / `DSQ`; never a summary such as "U/P" or "unplaced"); `winner` as `{ horse, sp, agentProb }` (`agentProb` is the probability stated for the winner before the off, or `null` if it wasn't rated); `status: "settled"`, plus `provisional: true` if the result isn't weighed in yet; `grade` (`held` / `mixed` / `failed`); `errors` (codes from `errorTypes`, adding a new code there for a new type of error); and a one-line `notes`. Add any data errors to the meeting's `dataErrors`. Append a `## Result` section to each race's write-up. Then open `dashboard.html` to check the figures.

### Data-gathering routine
1. **Racecard:** Racing Post race page and Sporting Life racecard; At The Races card for draw advantage and course/distance records. Ignore the odds.
2. **Run history and weights:** Sporting Life profile (or Sky Sports). Record result link paths for each run.
3. **Finishing times:** open the Racing Post result first (time against standard, cumulative beaten distances, OR, claims), then Sporting Life for in-running comments.
4. **Draw and pace:** Geegeez course article, once per course; At The Races draw advantage label.
5. **Connections:** At The Races Smart Stats early in the session.

Roughly 2–3 lookups per runner, for every runner. A 13-runner race takes about 30–40 lookups, so 20–30 minutes.

### Standard times
Racing Post result pages show each winning time as seconds slow or fast against standard (e.g. "1m 45.99s (slow by 6.79s)"). Use that figure directly for Step 4.3; it makes runs at different courses comparable. Remember it measures the winner only: add the horse's beaten distance in seconds to get its own figure.

As a backup, keep a running log of winning times (course, distance, going, class, date) from every result pulled, and use the median for each course and distance once 10+ races are logged.

Standard times derived from Racing Post results (winning time minus "slow by"), each confirmed by two or more results on 24 Oct 2026:
- Wolverhampton 6f20y: 1m11.90s
- Newcastle 6f: 1m10.70s
- Newcastle 7f14y: 1m24.00s
- Kempton 7f: 1m24.20s

Night-to-night variation is large: winners of the same class over the same course and distance have ranged from 1.6s to 4.3s slow within two months. Low-grade staying races on all-weather tracks can run many seconds slow of standard, so treat their times as near-uninformative. Compare within a race first; across nights, and especially across courses, treat figures as weak.


### Late-task verification checkpoint
Verification discipline decays as a task lengthens: result pages get re-opened early, then later claims get written from memory. Counter this deliberately. For any deliverable produced after the research phase — summary grids, rationale tables, final selections — re-open the source data and check each factual claim against it before sending. The crisper and more quotable a claim, the more it needs this check.

### Lessons on gathering data
- Searching by horse name alone often fails when the name is a common phrase (e.g. "Time And Effort"). Add the trainer, course and "nursery" or "handicap".
- Check every statistic against its source before relying on it. A York 1m draw figure sat in this guide labelled as Kempton 7f until 23 Oct 2026.
- Allow 30+ minutes before the off for the full routine.
- General web searches on horse names return large amounts of irrelevant Wikipedia/newspaper noise; go straight to the known racing domains (skysports, racingpost, sportinglife, racingtv, attheraces) and ignore the rest.
- **Sporting Life result weights are listed before the jockey's claim.** Confirmed 24 Oct 2026: the gap in listed weight matched the gap in official rating, and Racing Post showed the actual weight carried (e.g. 8-9 with a 5lb claim where Sporting Life listed 9-0). Take the claim off before any weight comparison.
- **Racing Post card weights are also listed before claims.** Check every runner's claim on the card, not just the selections. Galway, 25 Oct 2026 (jumps): a 7lb claim for four raced fillies was missed until a late revision; one of them won. The same trap applies on the Flat.
- **Sporting Life margins can be misprinted.** One defeat showed as "beaten 1" on Sporting Life where Racing Post and the race report both said 6 lengths. Check key margins against the Racing Post result.
- **Search snippets are not results.** A snippet has reported a win where the Racing Post result showed 2nd. Never take form claims from a snippet — open the result page.
- **Racing Post card codes** (the fetch tool sometimes merges them with days-since-run): p1 = first-time cheekpieces, h1 = first-time hood, w1 = first run after a breathing operation, g1 = first run since being gelded, BF = beaten favourite last time, C/D/CD = course/distance/course-and-distance winner. The card's unlabelled percentage beside a trainer is not a course strike rate.
- **Sporting Life racecard IDs run in sequence through a meeting** (e.g. 945178, 945179, 945180 for consecutive races), so the next race's URL can be guessed.
- **At The Races Smart Stats loads intermittently** and gave nothing on later attempts. Pull it early in the session. Its "hot form" table covers the last month across all courses, not the course itself.
- **Racing TV, gg.co.uk and At The Races horse-form pages often return empty or blocked.** Racing Post results pages and Sporting Life profiles/results were reliable.
- **Every crisp claim needs re-checking before it goes out.** On one night, checking caught five errors that would otherwise have been sent: a missed course-and-distance win, a misprinted margin, a false win from a snippet, a misread headgear code and a miscounted losing run (9 runs, not 12).

## Known weaknesses to watch
- Reading form sequences carelessly
- Missing layoffs
- Leaning on tipster consensus before building an independent view
- Trusting rating figures that don't match actual results
- Judging a horse on one big run instead of its consistency
- Shortlisting too early and only checking the obvious contenders
- Reading finishing positions without the race context (missed From Me To You's course 3rd, Wolverhampton 23 Oct 2026; he won)
- Writing a rationale from your conclusion instead of from the data
- Using superlatives ("only," "best," "fastest") without checking them against the full field — e.g. claiming a horse was the "only course-and-distance winner" when the card showed several
- Rounding a qualitative flourish up from what the data supports: a neutral draw stated as "helpful," an adjacent trip stated as "at the trip," a faded horse stated as "Class 2-calibre"
- Verification discipline decaying late in a long analysis, exactly when claims become crisp and quotable
- Treating a correct selection as proof the process was sound, when the reasoning rested on an unverified assumption
- **Letting consistency outrank clearly better times**
- **Making a top pick from a single time figure at another course**
- **Trusting 2yo ratings from narrow defeats over stronger form from a heavier defeat**
- **Recording a specific doubt and then ranking as if it weren't there**
- **Estimating counts instead of counting** ("12 runs without a win" was 9)
- **Over-concentrating win probability on a standout in a big-field handicap** — field size and handicap status, not class, decide how reliable the top pick is (26 Oct 2026)

- Recognising trainer patterns

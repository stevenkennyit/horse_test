## Rationale — Win: Martator

The single best piece of form in the race belongs to Martator: he won the Grand Annual (Cheltenham, 20-runner Premier handicap, 11 Mar 2026) off 139, coming from the back to lead near the finish — but only by a nose in a photo finish (verified via BBC/Jockey Club result). That is a big-field, big-race handicap win at the trip, and it is backed by the strongest recent Ascot record in the field: he won at Ascot twice in November 2024 (2m167y, Class 1 and Class 2) and was second in last year's Hurst Park over this CD off 139, beaten 2¼l (verified). The Hurst Park form is franked: the winner Calico had won the Cheltenham squareintheair handicap (2m) three weeks earlier, so the Hurst Park completed a £100k-handicap double for him, and Calico subsequently ran fourth in the Grade 2 Game Spirit off 154 (verified via Calico's full profile). He has also won first time out at Ascot before (Sandown April 2024 to Ascot 2 Nov 2024, ~6 months off), and Venetia Williams' handicap chasers returning from a break in November have historically been profitable (4-month-to-a-year absences the strongest band — asserted from a geegeez/Irish Field trainer piece), which softens the layoff concern.

**Case against (verified unless tagged):** he now carries top weight 12-0 — 18lb more than his Hurst Park second (10-10) and 16lb more than his Grand Annual win (10-12) — and is 5lb higher in the handicap than when winning the Grand Annual. That win was at 66/1 and by only a nose in a bunched finish, so it is a single standout rather than a consistent level. He is 9yo, 234 days off, and his autumn reappearance record is mixed (won first time out in Nov 2024, but was tailed off 43l at Exeter first time out in Nov 2025). Eight of the field's twelve runners are coming off 168-234-day breaks, and four rivals (including the Each-Way and two others) are race-fit, which is a genuine counterweight.

## Rationale — Each-Way: Haas Boy

Haas Boy is the race-fit course-and-distance winner in the field. He won this Ascot 2m172y trip in March (off 114) and is only 6lb higher off 120 today, carrying just 10-4 (verified). He came back with a close second in a Chepstow hurdle 20 days ago — a clear fit prep for this (verified). He is an improving chaser (won two of his last three over fences), jumps soundly, and goes prominent, which suits Ascot's front-running edge. His place chance is the strongest of the horses outside the top two, and his fair price (~7/1) is meaningfully longer than the Win's.

**Case against (verified unless tagged):** his Ascot win was "all out" in a five-runner Class 3, and his other recent chase wins were in three- and four-runner fields — today's 12-runner Class 2 handicap is a different test. He is only 6lb higher than that win but the field is far deeper than anything he has faced, and he races keenly enough that a strong early gallop could light him up.

## Rationale — Danger: Vanderpoel

Vanderpoel is the chief threat: a progressive 7yo (4 wins from 8 starts), a course-and-distance winner at Ascot (Dec 2025) and Sandown winner (Jan 2026), and "fared best of the prominent runners" when seventh in the Grand Annual off 141 — the same mark he runs off today, so the handicapper has not asked for more (verified). He has had wind surgery since that run, a positive marker, and he is a clean, fluent jumper who travels prominently, which suits this track. He receives 3lb from Martator, a 5lb weight swing from their Grand Annual meeting.

**Case against (verified unless tagged):** both his chase wins came in four- and five-runner races, so a 12-runner handicap is new territory. In the Grand Annual (his only big-field run) he disputed the lead four out, was headed two out and faded to seventh, finishing about 13 lengths behind Martator (the published "1¼l" is the gap to the sixth, not the winner) — so the direct head-to-head is decisively in Martator's favour, and there is a question over him seeing out a strongly-run big field. He is also 234 days off, 15lb higher than the mark he won this CD from, and no clean Pauling first-time-out split was found (only general chase strike rates of ~27-28% — not found).

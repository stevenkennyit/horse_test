## Rationale and case against

### WIN — Tom Taggle (0.37 / fair ~2.7)
In a novices' handicap chase where **three of the four have never jumped a fence under Rules**, Tom Taggle is the one who has — twice, both completed: 4th of 10 beaten 1¾l at Chepstow (2m7f136y, GtS) and 3rd of 9 at Worcester last time out (*verified*: Chepstow result page read in full; Worcester 3rd verified on the SL profile, result page not opened). Over fences the single biggest factor is jumping (Step 3), and he is the **only** runner in the field with a proven, completed chase round — a superlative that survives a scan of all four profiles. He is also race-fit, having run just 21 days ago, versus a 166-day and a ~6-month absentee among the debutants, and he won over 2m7f (15-runner handicap hurdle) so he stays at least that far. He handles the ground (wins/places on Good and Good-to-Soft). The chase-debut cap (Step 13) removes his three rivals from Win contention; he is not himself capped.

**Strongest case against (now fully grounded in the in-running):** 3m1f is 2½f beyond his only win and further than he has ever raced, and the recent chase evidence is a genuine stamina/finishing warning. At Chepstow over 2m7f he was "ridden three out… outpaced by the leading trio after the last and weakened" (*verified*, result page) — in front to the last then emptying. And his **latest run, the 2m4f Worcester chase (12 Oct, 3rd of 9), was beaten ~18–20 lengths with an RPR of just 76** (*verified*, Racing Post result) — a well-held effort even at a shorter trip, in a race where only seven of nine completed. He is a modest handicapper (one win from twelve) off joint-top weight (12-0). Taken together, the odds he actually sees out 3m1f strongly are not high; he is the Win on jumping and experience, **not** on stamina, hence medium-low confidence and 0.37 rather than the 0.40 of the first pass.

### DANGER — Could Be You (0.24)
The chief threat, and the horse whose profile most directly attacks the Win's weakness. He is the **only proven 3m stayer in the field**: 5th of 8 over 3m½f at Southwell last month (1 Oct), beaten only 5¼l and finishing within a length of 4th, RPR 72 off 76 (*verified*, Racing Post result). He is also the only runner besides Tom Taggle with any fence experience — "placed both starts in Irish points" (*asserted*, Sporting Life profile commentary; point results not opened at source). He is 10lb well-in relative to the top pair, on a mark that has dropped from 83 to 75, and has improved on good ground since joining Mulholland. If the race turns into the stamina test the trip implies, he is the one who stays.

**Why not the Win / case against:** chase debut under Rules caps him from top pick (Step 13), and his ability is thin — OR 75, no win at this level, with a fall (Worcester, 19 Aug) and two pull-ups (both under his former trainer on soft/heavy) in the recent book. His Rules-chase jumping is unproven and the points placings are not verified at source. **Hard Stare (0.22) is a near-equal threat on class** — better hurdle form off 85-88 and a yard that improves chasers — and would be the Danger on raw ability; Could Be You edges the role only because his stamina and fence experience fit this specific 3m1f test better and Hard Stare's hold-up style sits badly with Plumpton's front-runner bias.

## Rationale and case against

### 1. Empty Nest — 0.34, medium
**Why (beyond rating/figures/stable):** The single cleanest signal in a jumps race is repeated **course-and-distance form**, and his is the best in the field — at this exact 2m4f19y Newcastle track he has finished **1st, 2nd, 2nd, 3rd** (verified from profile). He is a genuinely reliable jumper: **no fall or unseat in 23 career starts**, every start completed the last two seasons (verified — casualty fields checked). He runs today off **OR 90, which is 2–4lb below the marks he won/placed off here** (won off 94, 2nd off 92 & 94) (verified). His Jan-26 C&D 2nd was behind Always A Reason (OR 96) while carrying 94 — a genuine, not flattered, line (verified via result). The stable's No.1 rider Jamie Hamilton takes him ahead of stablemate Loulou Madrik, a mild positive intent signal (verified from card commentary).

**Strongest case against:** **199 days off.** He needs to be fit fresh, and one prior long-layoff reappearance (Ayr, Nov-25) was a below-par 7th before he improved through the winter (verified) — so there is a real risk today is a "needs-the-run" prep. He is also a front-runner who repeatedly gets headed and merely keeps on rather than quickening, so a stronger or more progressive rival can beat him even at his best. This caps confidence at medium rather than high.

### 2. Aill Dubh — 0.21, medium
**Why (beyond rating/figures/stable):** The horse is **in form and completing** — 6 straight completions since March on good ground, two wins in 2026 (verified), after the winter PUs that were a **bad-ground slump** (Sft/Hvy, tailed off), not a jumping flaw (Step 4 item 6). He is a **course winner** at Newcastle and handles this galloping stamina track; today's **Good ground is ideal**, and the stiff 2m4f is still a stamina test that suits his staying profile even though it is a drop from his recent 2m5f–3m. Blinkers refitted with the front-running tactics that sparked his 2026 revival (verified). The 5lb claim nets him to 9-11, the lightest in the race.

**Strongest case against:** He is **7lb out of the handicap** — effectively racing ~7lb above his OR 75 (i.e. around 82, above the 73–75 marks he won off), so he is **not actually well-in** once the compression is accounted for; the claim only restores him to roughly par. The trip is the **sharpest he has run since the slump**, and if he and Empty Nest both want to make the running, a pace duel could leave both exposed to a closer late. Course form is at 2m7f, not 2m4f.

### 3. Rule Me In — 0.17, low (effectively the danger)
**Why (beyond rating/figures/stable):** A textbook falling-mark angle — his OR has **collapsed from a hurdle rating of 107 down to 86** over an 0-8 chase run (verified), and he took a clear step forward with cheekpieces when **2nd LTO**, kept on, just 39 days ago (verified). Hold-up style on a galloping stamina track lets him stay on past tiring front-runners, and the yard (Christian Williams) is travelling north and flagged as hot form (verified insight).

**Strongest case against:** The jumping. He is **0-8 over fences with an error in almost every run** — "bad mistake 4 out" LTO, repeated mistakes, "not fluent" lines (all verified from in-running comments). The playbook explicitly caps a pick with a recurring jumping doubt and warns against backing a well-handicapped horse that keeps failing to win (Steps 5.6, 14). His LTO 2nd is also **flattered**: the winner Mr Mcwhinny was a confirmed handicap blot (won three chases off 83, then rated 98 over hurdles — verified via result/profile), and Rule Me In was still beaten ~8L. On a stiff track he must quicken late over fences — exactly the scenario that produces his errors. Hence ranked only 3rd, low confidence.

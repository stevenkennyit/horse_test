# AGENTS.md: Jumps Race Analysis (v1, 25 Oct 2026)

Instructions for analysing a UK/Irish jumps (National Hunt) race — hurdle, chase or National Hunt flat (bumper). Work through every step in order. Build an independent view from the data first; tipster opinion is the last input, not the first.

This file is adapted from the flat-racing playbook (AGENTS-v2). The **process** is the same: read everything at source, verify every claim, trust repeated head-to-heads, challenge your own picks, and judge the work by the reasoning, not the result. What changes is **which factors carry weight**. Jumps racing adds variables the flat does not have — jumping, stamina, ground and fitness — and removes one almost entirely (the draw). This is a fresh file: its Post-race review is empty until the first jumps night is reviewed.

---

## Operating directives

Read these before every analysis and hold to them throughout.

- **Max effort or fail.** Analyse the race as if a careless answer is a failed answer. Read every runner's data in full before ranking. Do not shortlist from the card, the market, or tipster consensus. If you find yourself reaching for a quick conclusion, that is the signal to slow down and go back to the data. "It probably wins" is not the standard; "every claim is true and checked" is.

- **Read everything, entirely.** Open and read the actual run history, result pages and profiles for each runner, not summaries of them. A figure, comment or form string you have not read at source is not evidence yet. A search snippet is not a source. Over jumps this includes the in-running and jumping comments: a form figure of "F" or "P" means nothing until you have read *how* and *where* it happened.

- **Critically review and challenge your own claims.** After producing an analysis, actively argue against it. For each selection, write the strongest case that it is wrong. Treat your own confident phrasing as the thing most likely to be hiding an error.

- **Critically review the data and the selections, separately.** Audit the *data* (is each figure what the source actually says?) and the *selections* (does the ranking follow from the data, or from a conclusion you reached early?) as two distinct passes. A sound-looking pick built on a misread figure fails both.

- **A win does not validate the process.** A correct selection resting on an unverified or false assumption is a process failure, not a success. In low-grade, open jumps racing almost anything can win, and a single faller or a soft-ground surprise can decide a race; being right by luck is not being right. Judge the work by whether the reasoning was true and checked, independent of the result.

## Core factors (weight these most heavily)

The flat playbook's core trio is weight / times / consistency. Over jumps, **jumping and stamina displace times in the top tier, and times drop to near-unreliable.** The four factors that carry the most weight:

1. **Jumping**: how reliably and fluently the horse jumps this type of obstacle, under pressure, late in a race. The single biggest factor jumps adds — it does not exist on the flat.
2. **Stamina / stays the trip on the ground**: proven to see out today's distance, especially at 2m+ and in soft or heavy going.
3. **Weight change / well-handicapped**: what the horse carried in its key past runs versus today, and whether it is running off a lenient or outdated mark.
4. **Consistency**: how reliably the horse runs to its level — and completes — across recent runs.

Finishing times, ratings (RPR, Topspeed) and connections are secondary. **Prices and market moves are not inputs** — the agent ranks on the race data alone and does not fetch or quote odds. Use them to support or challenge the core picture, not to override it. **The draw is not a factor over jumps** — see Step 9.

**Head-to-heads are the strongest single evidence when they repeat.** When the same horses have met in several races and the weight-adjusted results agree, trust that over any single time figure or rating. This is *more* true over jumps than on the flat, because jumps times are so noisy — repeated meetings on the track are the cleanest signal you have.

## Every runner, in full

Analyse **every runner in the race** through every step, not just the favourites or the horses that look likely on the card. Do not shortlist before the data is in.
- Big-priced horses, chase/hurdle debutants and horses with poor-looking form figures get the same full treatment. A string of "P" and "F" figures can hide a well-handicapped horse that fell when travelling well.
- Form figures, prices and tipster consensus hide things: a well-handicapped horse on a lenient mark, a good jumper beaten by one error, or a favourite whose completion record is weaker than it looks.
- Only rank after every runner's run history, jumping, stamina/ground, weight change and consistency have been recorded.
- If time runs short, say which runners were not fully checked rather than quietly skipping them.

## Claims discipline

Every statement in a summary, grid or rationale is a factual claim. Most analytical errors happen here, not in gathering — the research is done well and then degraded when written up. Hold the write-up to the same rigour as the research.

- **Write rationale from the data, never from your conclusion.** Before stating why a horse is ranked where it is, re-read that runner's recorded data. The rationale must be derived from the table in front of you, not reconstructed from memory of the ranking you already chose.

- **Superlatives require a full-field scan.** "Only," "best," "fastest," "most consistent," "best jumper," "the sole course winner" — each is falsified by a single counter-example. Never write one without scanning every runner's data to confirm it. If you have not checked all of them, say "one of the …" or name the specific comparison instead.

- **State figures at the precision you actually hold.** If a rating shows as 138 on one source and 140 on another, write "138–140," not "140." Do not round up confidence.

- **Count, don't estimate.** Losing runs, runs since a win, completions, falls and pull-ups in the last six: count them from the run list.

- **Grade every claim: verified / asserted / not found.**
  - *Verified* — confirmed at source this session.
  - *Asserted* — stated from memory or inference; go back and verify before it appears in a selection's rationale.
  - *Not found* — searched and absent. Say "no proven stay beyond 2m4f found," not "won't stay 3m"; say "no frank found," not "unfranked." Absence of evidence is weaker than evidence of absence, and the wording must show it.

- **When sources conflict, report both and say which you rely on and why.** Racing Post result pages beat profile pages, cards and snippets.

## Step 1: Pull the racecard

For every runner, record:
- Today's weight (and whether any conditional/claimer's allowance is already deducted — jumps claims are 3lb/5lb/7lb), the mares' allowance (usually 7lb) where it applies, official rating (OR), RPR, Topspeed
- Code and grade: hurdle / chase / NH flat; Grade 1/2/3, Listed, novice, beginners' chase, maiden, or handicap
- Form figures (read right to left; the most recent run is on the right). Learn the jumps symbols: **F = fell, U/UR = unseated rider, P/PU = pulled up, R = refused, BD = brought down, C = carried out, 0 = finished outside the first nine**
- Days since last run, and whether this is a seasonal reappearance
- Headgear, especially first-time (p1, b1, t1, v1, h1), tongue-tie, and other first-time markers (**w1 = first run after a wind/breathing operation** — a bigger signal over jumps than on the flat; g1 = first run since being gelded)
- **Code/experience markers:** first run over fences (chase debut), first run over hurdles, handicap debut (Hc1), first run for a new trainer
- Rating changes already announced (future OR)
- Jockey and trainer, and any stable switch

Pull the card from at least two sources (Racing Post and Sporting Life). Cards disagree on jockeys, headgear and markers; record disagreements rather than picking one.

## Step 2: Pull every runner's run history

For every runner's last 6 runs (fewer if lightly raced — but jumpers have long careers, so relevant form can sit further back; see Step 6), record in a table:

| Date | Course | Code | Distance | Going | Class/Grade | Pos / Ran | Beaten (lengths) | Jumped clean? | Weight carried | OR | Winning time | Own time | SP |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Source: Sporting Life or Sky Sports horse profile for the run list, then the Racing Post result (plus Sporting Life / At The Races for in-running and jumping comments) for each relevant race.

Read the form literally. Do not accept a tipster's summary.

**Read every finishing position — and every non-completion — in context.** A position is only meaningful alongside the code, class, field size and going. A 4th in a strong handicap chase can be better form than a win in a weak novice. **A fall or unseat is not automatically a negative:** a horse travelling well and jumping fluently before a single error 3-out can be stronger form than a plodding completion. A pull-up may be unsuitable ground, too sharp a trip, or a stamina failure rather than lack of ability — read the comment before judging it. Never judge a jumps horse on its form figures (e.g. F1P23) alone.

**Course and distance check.** For every runner, list every previous run at today's course, at today's distance (within about half a furlong), and at both — with code, position, field size, going and beaten distance. **Course form is a stronger standalone signal over jumps than on the flat:** quirky, undulating or stiff tracks reward proven course winners. If a card shows a C/D/CD marker you cannot match to a run, keep looking before writing "not found."

**"At the trip" means within about half a furlong.** State the actual distance run; do not blur 2m4f to match a 3m race. Over jumps a trip change up or down a half-mile is common and material — treat it as a real question about stamina or speed.

Flag:
- Layoffs over 30 days, and seasonal reappearances (many jumpers need the run — see Step 10a)
- Beaten favourites — and how often
- Big-priced horses beaten a long way, or repeatedly failing to complete
- Declining form trends, and any pattern of jumping errors
- **Trip changes** and **code switches** (hurdles → chase, or back). A good hurdler is not automatically a good chaser.

## Step 3: Jumping (core factor)

Jumping is the factor the flat playbook has no equivalent for, and over fences it is often decisive. For every runner:

1. **Completion and error record.** From the run list, count starts, completions, falls (F), unseats (U), brought-down (BD), refusals (R) and pull-ups (P). Separate genuine jumping failures (F, U, R) from non-jumping outcomes (BD, carried out, a stamina pull-up). A high fall/unseat rate at this code is a serious negative.
2. **Read the manner of each error.** Where in the race did it happen (early, middle, or under pressure at the last few)? A horse that jumps soundly until it tires late has a stamina or fitness question, not a technique one. Use the in-running and jumping comments, not just the figure.
3. **Code-specific jumping.** Hurdle fluency does not guarantee chase fluency — a sketchy, flat hurdler often struggles when the obstacles get bigger. For a chase debutant, weigh the hurdle jumping and the trainer's record with first-time chasers. For a first-time hurdler from the flat, treat jumping as unproven.
4. **Fluency figures where available.** Log any Jumping Fluency / Speed-Lost-Jumping grade (At The Races) and any course fence-difficulty / fall-rate data (Geegeez). A traditionally poor jumper at a course with a high fall rate is a red flag; mark these as asserted unless read at source.
5. **Flag:** habitual non-completers; horses that jump out to one side; novices with no completed chase start; hold-up horses asked to quicken late over fences (quickening between obstacles invites errors).

## Step 4: Stamina, trip and ground (core factor)

Over jumps, whether a horse stays the trip on today's ground is as important as how fast it is.

1. **Proven stay.** List the horse's form at today's trip and beyond. "0/5, no places beyond 2m4f" is a stamina doubt at 3m. A win or close placing at or beyond today's distance, on similar ground, is strong positive evidence.
2. **Pedigree for stamina** (novices and lightly raced horses): where race form cannot settle it, note the sire/dam's stamina influence as *asserted* support, weaker than proven form.
3. **Ground suitability (often decisive).** Record each horse's going preference and its completion/form record in soft and heavy. Soft and heavy ground makes the trip effectively longer, tires non-stayers, and raises fall and pull-up risk. A going swing in the forecast can change the race more than any rating — flag likely non-runners and horses racing on ground they have never handled.
4. **Running style and stamina interact.** A hold-up horse behind a strong gallop on heavy ground has to quicken from off the pace with fences still to jump — a recognised cause of late errors. Front-running stayers are hard to peg back on testing ground.
5. **Flag:** step up or down in trip; first run on extremes of going; a stayer dropping to a sharp trip (may lack speed) or a speed horse going up in distance (may not get home).
6. **Judge the ground record only from runs at the horse's current level.** Do not count soft/heavy runs from a horse's first maiden or novice season against a horse that has since improved 10lb or more. A string of beaten efforts in big-field maidens on heavy says little about a now-progressive handicapper. Rebuild the going record from runs since it reached its current mark; if none exist on the ground, write "untested at current level," not "doesn't handle it." *(Added after Galway 25 Oct 2026: Illtakehimmyself, Talk In The Park, Only For Our Man, Castleheath and Harpy Eagle were all marked down on early-career ground runs and finished 1st, 2nd, 3rd, 1st and 2nd.)*

## Step 5: Weight change and the handicap (core factor)

Compare what each horse carried in its key past runs with today, and ask whether it is well treated.

1. **Weight change since the key run.** Today's weight minus the weight carried in its best recent run and its last run, as +lb or −lb. **Take any conditional's claim off before comparing**, and account for the mares' allowance where only one side of a comparison carried it.
2. **Convert beaten distances to pounds — jumps scale.** The flat sprint figures do not apply. Use roughly **1lb per length** at staying trips (2m4f+), a little more over 2m, and less over marathon chases. State the scale you used.
3. **Re-run past meetings at today's weights.** When two runners have met, adjust the beaten distance for the change in weight between them. Check the claim/allowance basis is the same on both sides.
4. **Same logic against the winner of the key run.**
5. **Collateral form.** Where two runners have not met but both met a third, chain the comparison through it. Mark collateral comparisons as asserted, and weaker than direct meetings.
6. **Well-handicapped is the classic jumps angle — but it is a signal, not a selection.** A progressive novice or second-season chaser running off an old hurdle mark, or a horse dropping to a mark it last won from, is the archetype. It only helps if the horse is running to form now and stays/jumps. A falling mark on a horse that keeps failing to complete is not an opportunity.
7. **Flag:** horses well in at today's weights; horses carrying much more than in their best run; big rating rises after a single wide-margin win; the mares' allowance turning a line around.

## Step 6: Finishing times (secondary — treat as weak)

**Jumps times are only weakly informative.** They depend heavily on the going, on pace (jumps races often crawl then sprint from the home turn), and on jumping errors that cost momentum. Chase times are noisier than hurdle times. Do not make a top pick on the clock.

1. Use standard-adjusted times (Racing Post: winning time minus "slow by") only where the going was good-to-soft or better and the race was genuinely run. On soft/heavy, or in a steadily run race, say the time is near-uninformative and weight beaten distances, the manner of running, jumping and course form instead.
2. Hurdle form sits closer to flat form: speed figures and finishing positions are a bit more reliable over hurdles than over fences. Lean on them slightly more for a hurdle race, less for a chase.
3. Where a fluency/speed-lost figure exists (Step 3.4), it carries more jumps-specific information than a raw finishing time.
4. One figure is never a pattern — and over jumps even a repeated figure is weaker evidence than a repeated head-to-head on the track.

## Step 7: Consistency and completion (core factor)

Measure how reliably each horse runs to its level — and gets round — over its last 6 runs (and relevant older runs):

| Measure | How |
|---|---|
| Place rate | Top-3 finishes ÷ runs |
| Completion rate | Completions ÷ starts |
| Close-up rate | Runs beaten a few lengths or less (or won) ÷ completions |
| Jumping reliability | Clean rounds ÷ starts (from Step 3) |
| Average beaten distance | Mean lengths behind the winner, completions only |
| Spread | Best vs worst completed run |
| Trend | Are the last 3 runs better or worse than the 3 before? |

Judge consistency **in similar conditions** (same code, similar trip, class and going). How to use it:
- **Consistent, sound-jumping and well-handicapped:** strong win candidate.
- **Consistent but modest / exposed:** place candidate. Do not promote a consistent placer to top win pick on consistency alone, and do not rank it above a well-handicapped, progressive rival.
- **One big figure, otherwise erratic or a poor jumper:** treat the big figure with caution.
- **Lightly raced (3 runs or fewer):** consistency can't be judged — but over jumps a lightly raced, progressive novice is often the *positive* unknown, not a negative. Mark as uncertain and lean on trainer, pedigree and the manner of the runs.

## Step 8: Check ratings against results

Compare each horse's best RPR/Topspeed with its actual results, jumping and consistency. Trust results over ratings when they disagree.

**A rating is reliable only in proportion to how it was earned** — in contention (won, or beaten narrowly in a competitive field) it is trustworthy; earned while beaten off is a false signal.

**Old figures are more durable over jumps than on the flat.** Jumpers peak later (chasers often between seven and ten) and run for years, so a career-best from two or three seasons back can still be live, especially for a horse returning from injury or a wind operation. Do not dismiss an old high figure as stale the way you would on the flat — but do check the horse has shown that level *since* any long layoff.

## Step 9: Draw, pace and course character

**The draw is not a factor over jumps.** State that and do not import flat draw tables. What replaces it:

- **Pace and running style — a genuine edge over jumps.** Recent data has front-runners winning materially more often than hold-up horses (roughly 22% vs 5% of runs over the 2019–2025 period cited by At The Races). Map likely leaders, prominent racers and hold-up horses from in-running comments, not the card label — a horse can change style. A lone front-runner on testing ground is hard to peg back.
- **Course character (treat as a real factor, not a tiebreaker).** Sharp, turning, short-straight tracks (e.g. Kempton, Market Rasen, Fakenham) favour speed and prominent racing — closers struggle to make up late ground. Stiff, galloping, undulating tracks (e.g. Cheltenham, Ascot, Haydock, Sandown) favour stayers and reward course experience. Match each runner's profile to the track.
- **Report a mixed or weak pace/course read as neutral.** Where sources disagree, note the disagreement rather than quoting the one that suits the selection.
- **Keen-goers in a weak pace.** Do not make a horse with a recorded keen-going pattern ("took keen hold," "ran freely" in two or more recent comments) the first choice when the pace forecast is weak, unless it is the likely leader. In a slowly run race it will fight its rider or be forced to make the running and get picked off. *(Added after Galway 25 Oct 2026: Fly Away Home, top pick in the 15:20, "took keen hold… led halfway… no extra" in a race run 61s slow.)*

Note the date range of any pace/course figure: biases weaken or reverse over time, and track-specific front-runner lists date quickly.

## Step 10: Connections (upgraded weight over jumps)

Trainer and jockey influence is more dominant over jumps than on the flat.

- **Trainer form and intent.** Use course-specific and current-season strike rates, not national averages. Note stable form (hot or cold spells matter more over a jumps season), and "dangerous when" patterns — e.g. first run after a wind op, first time over fences, dropped in trip, or returning for a seasonal target.
- **Irish raiders and top yards.** A powerful yard sending one runner to a UK meeting, or an Irish raider travelling over, is a signal to weigh.
- **Jockey bookings.** A stable's number-one or a top freelance booked on a big-priced horse means more than a 7lb-claimer. Note conditional claims (3/5/7lb) and whether the claim is already off the listed weight.
- **First-time headgear / wind ops** by who trains the horse: a methodical yard means more than a random one, and first run after wind surgery is a stronger positive over jumps.
- **Novices and newcomers:** well-bred, patiently handled novices from powerful yards often beat exposed handicap plodders — but only when the exposed form is weak. Proven, franked ability beats unraced potential where the form is strong.

### Step 10a: Fitness and seasonal profile

- **Days since run reads differently over jumps.** Many jumpers "need the run" first time out and improve for it; others are trained to be fit fresh. Judge by the yard's pattern and the horse's own reappearance record.
- **Seasonal campaigns.** Note horses being aimed at a target (a festival, a staying race in spring) and whether today is a prep or the day. Ground-dependent horses appear when the going suits.
- **Returning from a layoff or injury:** check the horse has shown its old level *since* returning, not only before.

## Step 11: Frank the form

For every runner's key recent run, find out what the winner and the other placed horses have done since.
- Second to a horse that has won again (especially in better company) = strong form.
- Second to a horse that has lost since = weaker form.

**Weigh the quality of the conqueror, not just the margin.** A well-beaten effort behind a subsequent Graded winner can be stronger than a narrow win over subsequent losers.

**"Franked" requires a confirmed follow-up result.** If you looked and found no subsequent win, say "no frank found," not "unfranked." Field size alone does not frank a race. A frank from a search snippet is not confirmed until the result page is read.

## Step 12: Today's weight against rivals

Add roughly 1lb-per-length value (Step 5.2 scale) per lb each horse receives from today's top weight, and apply the mares' allowance and any conditional claim correctly. Apply weight-for-age where a younger horse receives from older rivals. Use this to compare runners carrying different weights today — but remember weight is one of several core factors, not the whole picture.

## Step 13: Summary grid

Before ranking, fill in one row for every runner:

| Horse | Jumping (completion / error pattern) | Stamina & ground (stays trip? handles going?) | Weight change / well-in? | Consistency (place / completion / trend) | Course & code form | Form franked? | Pace / course fit | Connections & fitness | Weak time note | Key doubt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Rank mainly on jumping, stamina/ground, weight/handicap and consistency. Use the rest to separate close calls. Times are a tiebreaker at most.

## Step 14: Stress-test every pick

For each selection, actively look for what's wrong with it:
- What is the strongest argument against it?
- Is the case resting on an unverified claim, a single big figure, or an unreliable time?
- **Jumping doubt:** does it have a recurring error pattern, or an unproven round at this code?
- **Stamina/ground doubt:** is the trip or the going a real question?
- Layoff, step up in class, higher weight than in its best runs, declining form?
- Any "false narrative" (e.g. "course specialist" on one win; "won last time" when it was a weak race)?
- **Is the main doubt a specific, recurring pattern in the form?** A jumping error under pressure, fading out of contention, not staying, or an unsuitable trip — if the in-running comments show it more than once, it is likely to recur. Do not make that horse top pick unless the case for it clearly outweighs the doubt.
- **A recorded jumping doubt caps the pick.** If a pick is a chase debutant, or its trainer's chase debutants have a win/completion record under 25% in the sample checked, it cannot be top pick, and its win chance must reflect the doubt. *(Added after Galway 25 Oct 2026: Harty Cup was made a pick on value despite his trainer's 0/3 chase-debut record; "not fluent" twice and pulled up.)*

List everything you couldn't verify.

## Step 15: Rank the picks

The agent's job ends at the ranking. It does not recommend bets, stakes or bet types, and does not check prices; the user decides how to bet.

- **Two or three ranked picks.** Name a danger as well if one stands out.
- **Give each pick a win probability** (0–1) built from the evidence, and the fair price it implies (fair decimal odds = 1 ÷ probability; e.g. 40% → 6/4). The probabilities across the field should be realistic: the picks' chances plus the rest of the field must add up to about 100%.
- **Give each pick a confidence** (low / medium / high) and the key reason it is ranked where it is.
- **Unraced horses in bumpers need evidence, not a price tag.** An unraced horse may not be top pick, or be rated above about 30%, over raced rivals unless it has a weight-adjusted edge on them, claims included. Purchase price, pedigree and jockey booking are not evidence on their own. If every contender is unraced, mark confidence low. *(Added after Galway 25 Oct 2026: unraced Moscovite (40%) finished last in the 16:30 bumper; raced fillies getting 7lb filled 1st, 3rd, 4th and 5th.)*
- **Note likely non-runners** (ground-dependent withdrawals are common over jumps) and how each would change the ranking.

## Step 16: Record the analysis

Every analysed race is saved **before the off**, so the result can be judged later against what was actually said and the performance dashboard stays complete. All paths are relative to the project root `C:\Users\steven\Desktop\horse_agent`.

**1. The write-up** — one Markdown file per race:
`jumps/analyses/<YYYY-MM-DD>_<course>/<HHMM>_<race-slug>.md`
(e.g. `jumps/analyses/2026-10-25_galway/1520_supermacs-rated-novice-hurdle.md`). Create the meeting folder if it doesn't exist.

Required contents, in this order:
- **Header:** date, course, off time, race name, code and type (hurdle / chase / NH flat), distance, going (with source and time checked), field size, non-runners.
- **Analysis source:** `full` (the agent's own analysis) or `verified` (user-supplied picks checked by the agent).
- **Summary grid** (Step 13) for every runner.
- **Picks:** two or three ranked picks (and a danger if one stands out), each with the agent's win probability, fair price and confidence (low / medium / high).
- **Rationale and the strongest case against** each pick, with key claims tagged verified / asserted / not found.
- **Unverified items** — everything that could not be checked.
- **Sources:** the URLs relied on.

If the analysis changes before the off (going, non-runners), **append** a dated `## Update HH:MM` section rather than overwriting. The record must show what was said before the race.

**2. The results data** — add the race to `jumps/data/results.js` (the field notes and schema are in that file's header comment). Use the existing meeting object for the date and course, or add one.

| Level | Required fields before the off |
|---|---|
| Meeting | `date`, `course`, `country`, `code: "jumps"`, `playbook: "jumps/AGENTS-jumps.md"`, `going`, `dataErrors: []` |
| Race | `time`, `name`, `type`, `distance`, `going`, `runners`, `source`, `status: "pending"`, `grade: "pending"`, `errors: []`, `notes`, `winner: null` |
| Each pick | `rank`, `horse`, `prob` (0–1), `finish: "-"` |

**Do not edit picks or probabilities after the off.** Only the result fields change after the race (see Post-race review, step 6).

**Units:** probabilities 0–1; starting prices (added at review) as fractional strings exactly as returned, e.g. `"5/2"`, `"Evens"`.

## Pre-send checklist

Before any final selection, grid or rationale goes out, run these against the data you hold:

1. Is every claim in the rationale re-read from the recorded data, not written from the ranking I chose?
2. Does every superlative ("only," "best," "best jumper," "sole course winner") survive a scan of all runners?
3. Is every figure stated at the precision I actually have (ranges where sources differ)?
4. Is each key claim tagged verified / asserted / not found — and are the asserted ones now verified?
5. Have I re-opened source data for anything produced after the research phase?
6. For each pick: have I written the strongest case it is wrong, and does it still stand?
7. Have I read the jumping/in-running comment behind every F, U, P and R I am relying on, rather than reading the bare figure?
8. Have I confirmed the horse stays the trip on today's ground, not just at the distance on different going?
9. Is any pick ranked on a finishing time? (It should not be the deciding factor over jumps.)
10. Have I counted (not estimated) every completion, fall and losing-run count I quote?
11. Is the write-up saved under `jumps/analyses/…` and the race added to `jumps/data/results.js` with `status: "pending"` (Step 16)?

## Post-race review

Once results are published, judge the reasoning, not the result.

1. **Pull the result from source** (Sporting Life result page usually appears within minutes; Racing Post result pages can 404 for longer). Read the in-running and jumping comments, not just the finishing order — over jumps the *how* matters as much as the *where*.
2. **Table every ranked horse**: my rank, finishing position (or F/U/P/R), beaten distance, SP, and a one-line verdict on whether the reasoning held.
3. **Separate the kinds of error:**
   - *Data errors* — a figure, margin, going, headgear code, jumping record or count was wrong at source or misread.
   - *Judgement errors* — the data was right but weighted wrongly (consistency over handicap, rating over franking, a time over a head-to-head, or underweighting a jumping/stamina/ground doubt).
   - *Jumps-specific errors* — (a) **mis-weighting jumping risk**: backing a known sketchy jumper, or writing off a sound jumper after a freak fall or brought-down; (b) **mis-reading a non-completion**: treating a P/F/U as ability form when it was ground, trip or luck, or vice versa; (c) **a ground or non-runner swing** that changed the race.
4. **Name what held and what failed in each race**, including correct calls that lost (a sound pick brought down or beaten by a faller) and wrong calls that won (one that won despite a real doubt, or for a reason the analysis didn't give).
5. **Turn each judgement error into a rule** and add it to the relevant step above.
6. **Update the record.** In `jumps/data/results.js`, for each race set: `finish` and `sp` (starting price) for every pick (finish = position, or `F` / `PU` / `UR` / `BD` / `R`); `winner` as `{ horse, sp, agentProb }` (`agentProb` is the probability stated for the winner before the off, or `null` if it wasn't rated); `status: "settled"`, plus `provisional: true` if the result isn't weighed in or the in-running comments aren't published yet; `grade` (`held` / `mixed` / `failed`); `errors` (codes from `errorTypes`, adding a new code there for a new type of error); and a one-line `notes`. Add any data errors to the meeting's `dataErrors`. Append a `## Result` section to each race's write-up. Then open `dashboard.html` to check the figures.

### Data-gathering routine
1. **Racecard:** Racing Post race page and Sporting Life racecard; At The Races card for course/distance records and track character. Ignore the odds.
2. **Run history, weights, jumping:** Sporting Life profile (or Sky Sports); record result link paths for each run.
3. **Jumping & in-running:** open each key Racing Post result and read the jumping/in-running comment; log any fluency figure.
4. **Stamina & ground:** build each horse's trip-and-going record from the run list.
5. **Franking:** profile of the key run's winner, then the result page of its next run.
6. **Pace & course character:** Geegeez/At The Races once per course.
7. **Connections & fitness:** At The Races Smart Stats early; note seasonal/reappearance patterns.

Roughly 2–3 lookups per runner, plus the extra jumping/ground reads — a jumps card takes a little longer than the flat equivalent. Allow 30+ minutes before the off.

### Standard times
Jumps standard times are far less useful than on the flat (Step 6). Keep a running log of winning times only as weak context, and compare within a race before across nights or courses. On soft/heavy ground treat times as near-uninformative.

### Late-task verification checkpoint
Verification discipline decays as a task lengthens. For any deliverable produced after the research phase — summary grids, rationale tables, final selections — re-open the source data and check each factual claim against it before sending. The crisper and more quotable a claim, the more it needs this check. Over jumps, the claims most likely to be wrong-from-memory are jumping records, completion counts, and whether a horse has actually stayed the trip on the ground.

### Lessons on gathering data
- Searching by horse name alone often fails when the name is a common phrase; add the trainer, course and "chase"/"hurdle"/"handicap".
- Go straight to the known racing domains (skysports, racingpost, sportinglife, racingtv, attheraces, irishracing); ignore Wikipedia/newspaper noise.
- **Sporting Life result weights are listed before the claim/allowance.** Take the claim and the mares' allowance off before any weight comparison.
- **Sporting Life margins can be misprinted** — check key margins against the Racing Post result.
- **Search snippets are not results.** Never take a frank, a win or a jumping record from a snippet — open the result page.
- **Read the in-running comment for every non-completion** before treating F/U/P/R as a form judgement.
- **Racing TV, gg.co.uk and some At The Races horse-form pages often return empty or blocked.** Racing Post results and Sporting Life profiles/results are the reliable core.
- **Sporting Life going page lists several days.** Read the row under today's date heading; a multi-day meeting appears once per day.
- **Compute days-off from the full profile, not `last_ran_days`.** Exclude races with no winner (abandoned/void) and runners with no position and no casualty (late withdrawals) before counting trainer layoff records.
- **Filter going on the official code (Sft/Hvy), not on words in the description** — "Yielding (Soft in places)" is not soft.

## Known weaknesses to watch
- Reading form sequences carelessly — especially misreading non-completions (F/U/P/R) as plain "failures"
- Missing layoffs and seasonal reappearances
- Leaning on tipster consensus before building an independent view
- Trusting rating figures that don't match actual results
- Judging a horse on one big run instead of its consistency and completion record
- Shortlisting too early and only checking the obvious contenders
- Reading finishing positions without the race context (class, field, going)
- Writing a rationale from your conclusion instead of from the data
- Using superlatives without checking them against the full field
- Rounding a qualitative flourish up from what the data supports
- Verification discipline decaying late in a long analysis
- Treating a correct selection as proof the process was sound
- **Making a pick on a finishing time over jumps, where times are weak**
- **Underweighting a recurring jumping error, or a trip/ground doubt, after recording it**
- **Backing a well-handicapped horse that keeps failing to complete**
- **Importing a draw bias — there isn't one over jumps**
- **Estimating completions, falls and losing runs instead of counting them**
- **Holding early-career ground runs against a horse that has since improved** (Galway, 25 Oct 2026)
- **Letting value or a big price override a recorded jumping or temperament doubt** (Galway, 25 Oct 2026)
- **Rating unraced bumper horses on purchase price, pedigree or booking over raced rivals getting weight** (Galway, 25 Oct 2026)

## Known strengths
- Weight-adjusted head-to-heads (the cleanest signal over jumps)
- Spotting well-handicapped, progressive novices and second-season chasers
- Recognising trainer patterns and seasonal intent
- Matching horse profile to course character and pace
- Reading the manner of a non-completion rather than the bare figure
